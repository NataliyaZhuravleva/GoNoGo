# Mountaineering Conditions Evaluation Guidelines

This skill provides expert guidelines for translating raw weather parameters (temperature, precipitation, wind, cloud cover, visibility, and freezing level) at various elevation tiers into mountaineering safety decisions.

## 1. Wind Exposure and Ridge Hazards
*   **Ridges & Summits vs. Trailheads**: Wind speed increases dramatically with elevation and exposure. Sheltered approaches (base/mid tiers) may feel calm, while peaks are subjected to severe gusts.
*   **Thresholds**:
    *   **Calm (< 15 mph)**: Safe and manageable.
    *   **Moderate (15 - 30 mph)**: Challenging, increases balance loss risk on narrow ridges, and introduces significant wind chill. Proceed with caution.
    *   **High (> 30 mph)**: Dangerous. High risk of being blown off balance on exposed terrain, extreme wind chill/frostbite risk, and high navigation difficulty.

## 2. Freezing Level vs. Elevation (Freeze-Thaw & Rockfall)
*   **The Crucial Freeze-Thaw Cycle**: The freezing level (height at which temperature is 0°C / 32°F) determines snowpack stability and rockfall hazards.
*   **Freezing Level Below Tier**: The snow is frozen and firm. Excellent for glacier travel and steep snow climbing.
*   **Freezing Level Above Tier**:
    *   **Mildly Warm (< 1,000 ft above)**: Minor softening of snow, generally safe for summer mountaineering.
    *   **Moderately Warm (1,000 - 3,000 ft above)**: Significant melting. Snow becomes mushy (causing postholing and fatigue) and snow bridges over crevasses weaken.
    *   **Dangerously Warm (> 3,000 ft above)**: Extreme melt. High risk of wet-slab or wet-loose avalanches, and rapid destabilization of rock/ice leading to severe rockfall hazards on volcanic peaks (e.g., Mt. Rainier, Mt. Adams, Mt. Hood).

## 3. Precipitation Type and Intensity
*   **Rain vs. Snow**:
    *   **Rain (Temp > 32°F / 0°C)**: Rain at any mountaineering tier is a critical hazard. It wets out clothing rapidly (high hypothermia risk), degrades visibility, and severely destabilizes the snowpack.
    *   **Snow (Temp <= 32°F / 0°C)**: Snow is acceptable in light amounts, but heavy accumulation (> 1.0 mm/h) reduces visibility, increases avalanche hazard, and covers routes/tracks.

## 4. Cloud Cover, Visibility, and Navigation Risk
*   **Whiteouts**: Overcast skies (> 80% cloud cover) combined with low visibility (< 3 km) create whiteout conditions. In alpine zones without trees, navigation becomes nearly impossible, leading to disorientation, fall hazards, and crevasse falls.
*   **Navigation Hazards**: Even if wind and temperature are benign, dense cloud cover and low visibility alone represent high navigation risks.
